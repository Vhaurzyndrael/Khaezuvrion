# Khaezuvrion

> A violet thread sews the horizon to the underside of a forgotten wing.

## The Tailor of Empty Sleeves

The tailor works in a tent pitched beneath an arch of black stone. There is no town nearby. His customers arrive carrying garments that belong to nobody: a coat with the shape of an absent shoulder, a dress remembering a dance that never occurred, a glove whose fingers curl around an invisible ring.

He accepts no coins. Payment is made in distances. One customer paid with the length of a road between two vanished houses. Another offered the space between a waking thought and its first word. The tailor measured both with the same violet thread.

At the back of the tent stands a mannequin wrapped in pale cloth. It has no head. Its shadow has antlers. Nobody asks why, because the shadow lowers its head whenever a question begins.

## A Needle Taken From the Sky

The tailor's needle was found after a storm in which lightning remained suspended above the plain. A shepherd climbed the tallest tree and broke off a small piece. By morning, the fragment had cooled into a slender needle with an eye shaped like a crescent moon.

The needle will pass through cloth, leather, stone, and reflections. It will not pass through anything whose owner is thinking of it. The tailor therefore asks customers to remember a distant room while he works.

One woman could remember no room except the one in which she stood. The tailor sewed her sleeve to its own reflection. When she left, her reflection remained seated beside the mannequin. It occupied the tent for nine nights, although no mirror was present.

On the tenth night, the reflection stood up and folded itself into a square. The tailor placed it beneath a stack of unfinished collars.

## The Cloth Beneath the Cloth

Visitors sometimes notice a second tent visible through the seams of the first. Its fabric is blue, its arch white, and its mannequin complete. In that tent the tailor works with his eyes closed, using thread that shines only in shadow.

There is no way to enter it by lifting the fabric. The seams reveal it only when left undisturbed. Pulling a seam apart shows ordinary darkness and the far side of the plain.

The tailor says that the other tent belongs to an hour that has not arrived. Asked when it will arrive, he measures the visitor's sleeve and adds another stitch.

Every stitch changes something on the plain. A stone turns over. A patch of grass bends toward yesterday's wind. An animal pauses beneath the arch and watches its shadow pass through without it.

## The Wing Behind the Horizon

The violet thread extends farther than its spool permits. Once, a customer followed it from the tent to the horizon. There it disappeared beneath the earth, but its shadow continued across the sky.

He waited until evening. The sky darkened except for a vast shape above the plain, a folded wing outlined by small points of amber. The points were scales. Each scale held the reflection of a different horizon, and beneath every reflected horizon stood the tailor's tent.

The customer returned with his boots full of white sand. He had crossed no sand on his journey. The tailor poured it into a bowl and found a small tooth among the grains. Its root was wrapped in violet thread.

He sewed the tooth into the lining of the customer's coat. Thereafter, the coat was always warm on the side facing the horizon, regardless of which direction its wearer walked.

## A Pattern With No Garment

Under the mannequin lies a pattern cut from thin silver. Its pieces fit together only when nobody looks at them. From one angle they resemble a cloak. From another they resemble the bones of a bird. Held against the light, they form a doorway.

Along one edge are four marks:

*Khae — zuv — rion — othal.*

The tailor places a pin through each mark before sunrise. At dusk, all four pins have moved. They now point toward the unfinished collars beneath which the woman's reflection was folded.

He has never unfolded it. Once, however, a collar rose slightly from the stack. Beneath it came the sound of somebody breathing against glass. The mannequin's shadow lowered its antlers and remained motionless until dawn.

## The Last Measurement

A visitor arrived without a garment. He carried only an empty spool and asked the tailor to measure the distance to something he had forgotten. The tailor laid the violet thread across his palm. It became a narrow bridge extending through the tent wall.

Beyond the wall stood the blue tent. The other mannequin had opened its eyes. Its face resembled every customer who had ever entered, but its gaze belonged to something sleeping above the plain.

The visitor stepped onto the bridge. His shadow stayed behind. The tailor wound the thread around the empty spool until the bridge was gone. In the visitor's place remained a sleeve filled with white sand.

The tailor shook out the sand, folded the sleeve, and placed it beside the silver pattern.

The tent still stands beneath the arch.

The mannequin still waits beneath its cloth.

And somewhere beyond the last measurement, a folded wing grows warmer with every stitch.
